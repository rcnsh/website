# 0145. Cover tone curve: percentile stretch and mean-seeking gamma

A cover's luminance must spread across the field's five levels. `src/lib/lattice/tone.ts` stretches it between its 2nd and 98th percentiles (`low: 0.02`, `high: 0.98`), then applies the gamma, found by bisection and bounded to 0.4–1.25 (`minGamma`, `maxGamma`), that brings its mean to 0.28 (`mean`). The code is pure, so `src/lib/lattice/cover.ts` runs it on pixels and the tests run it on numbers.

A min-to-max stretch let one specular highlight or a black border squeeze the rest of a cover into one or two levels; percentiles ignore them. A mean-seeking gamma lifts a dark sleeve's mid-tones across the levels while a cover already bright in places (a black bird on yellow) is lifted far less, because its highlights carry the mean. The gamma bounds stop noise in a near-black cover being blown up and keep a bright cover from being darkened much. The target of 0.28 is lower than a cover looked at on its own would want, because the dissolve keeps bright triangles longest and the ordered dither spreads mid-tones up a level; at 0.35 a dark sleeve came out lighter than it feels and black-on-yellow (Trench) lost its black. It is tuned together with the dissolve band and the dither amplitude.

A cover whose own 2nd–98th percentile range spans less than 6% of the full range (`flat`) is drawn at its real brightness, and the stretch fades in fully by 30% (`full`) on a smoothstep: an all-black sleeve (Donda) has nothing to stretch, and stretching it anyway lit the whole field; a white one likewise stays white. The bottom 8% of the stretched range (`toe`) is held black by a smoothstep toe, because a gamma under 1 is steepest at zero and would otherwise lift the grain in a black background (the Discovery sleeve) a whole level. Luminance is Rec. 601 luma (0.299, 0.587, 0.114), `luminance` in `tone.ts`, restated in GLSL in `src/lib/lattice/shader.ts`, so the curve is measured on the same luminance the shader levels by; change both together.
