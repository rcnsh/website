# 0137. One fragment shader on the triangle lattice

The lattice is equilateral triangles with horizontal bases and slanted edges leaning right at 60°; the rcn mark is exactly 9 sides wide and 3 rows tall on it. The field is one full-screen triangle and one fragment shader (`src/lib/lattice/shader.ts`) that finds each pixel's lattice triangle and paints all of it one of the five levels. In lattice coordinates v counts rows and u counts sides along a row, shifting half a side per row, so a unit cell is a rhombus split into two triangles by its short diagonal u = v. Placement lives in pure TypeScript (`src/lib/lattice/geometry.ts`) so it tests under `node --test`; the GLSL cannot import it and restates the same coordinates, so the two change together.

A glyph is read at the triangle's centre and a cover is averaged over its area. Glyph edges are lattice edges, so averaging would grey them. A cover is read at the centroids of the n² equal-area sub-triangles (`triangleTaps(3)`, nine taps) and takes their plain mean; equal areas make that mean an estimate of the picture's mean over the triangle, so a stray pixel cannot decide a triangle and detail finer than a triangle evens out instead of speckling.

Glyph pictures (mark, text) are drawn in greys on a transparent canvas: brightness gives the level and alpha says whether the shape is there at all, so a glyph's off-shape triangles stay empty rather than drawing level 0. In the mark texture (`drawMark`) the first logo polygon (indigo, `#5b66af`) is white and the second (blue, `#305b99`) is `#b4b4b4`, which keeps the order of the mark's own two tones: indigo at the field's top level, blue one level below.
